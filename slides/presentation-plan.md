# Four-Minute Classroom Progress Report Plan

## Purpose

This is the team's Stage 1 progress report, not a complete lecture. A prerecorded
video will be played in class. It must show what the team is developing, concrete
work already completed, one representative teaching example, the main problem found
in the earlier version, and the next priorities for Stage 2.

## Required format

- **Format:** one prerecorded MP4 report played from a local computer or USB drive.
- **Maximum duration:** 4 minutes, including opening and closing.
- **Target duration:** approximately 3:40–3:50.
- **Submission folder:** `TeamName_Stage1_StatusReport`.
- **Offline rule:** the report must not depend on GitHub, cloud storage, logins, or a live Internet connection.
- **Evidence rule:** use readable screenshots or screen recordings of the repository and Markdown, not claims without visible evidence.

## Evidence to capture before recording

1. A repository tree showing `content/`, `project-management/`, `slides/`, and `videos/`.
2. One meaningful change or commit showing improvement to the project structure or teaching content.
3. A readable excerpt from the Hardware worked example and its answer key.
4. A local diagram or slide showing the ADD/LOAD trace.
5. The current project plan or task list showing the revision and next steps.

Screenshots must be captured in advance and included in the video. Do not open GitHub
live during the classroom report.

## Suggested narration

### Opening and direction — 0:00–0:40

“What happens between a sensor reading and an agent's action? Our project answers that
question through three connected beginner modules: Hardware, Networks, and Behaviourist
AI. We are designing the materials for students who have little or no previous
knowledge of these topics.”

### Progress and evidence — 0:40–1:45

“The repository now separates the three content areas and includes outlines, answer
keys, local diagrams, project-management records, slide materials, and video
storyboards. This excerpt is not a heading or a plan: it is a worked explanation with
a learner activity and an explained answer. The change history and local assets show
how the material was developed and made usable offline.”

### Representative example — 1:45–2:55

“In the Hardware example, the learner follows an ADD instruction. The CPU fetches the
instruction, decodes its operands, and the ALU calculates the result. The learner is
then asked to predict the new register value before seeing the answer. The same
trace-based method continues into an HTTPS request in Networks and a Q-learning update
in Behaviourist AI: identify the state, follow the transition, and explain the result.”

### Problem and next steps — 2:55–4:00

“Our important weakness was architectural. We had invested heavily in topic content,
but the framework, continuation plan, review evidence, and presentation structure were
not clear. After the 25 October B1 review, we reorganised the project plan, tasks, QA
records, module structure, slides, and video materials. For Stage 2, due 7 November,
we will complete the independent content review, integrate the three modules into the
Study Guide, and improve the classroom media. The result we want learners to remember
is simple: follow the data, identify the responsible component, and explain how the
system changes.”
